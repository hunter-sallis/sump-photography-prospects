The big insight

Federal photography is usually hidden inside larger contracts

The Alabama research found that the federal government mostly buys visual information, multimedia, communications support and public affairs, rather than "photography" by name. On construction, the photos are usually bought by the builder, not the agency.

Principles

How to write the first email

  1. Keep the first email short

    A few sentences. No long bio. A project manager should read it in under a minute.

  2. Use a subject line that says what you do and why it's relevant

    Name the project and the service — e.g. “Progress photography — Army Reserve Center, Birmingham.”

  3. Say who you are in one or two lines

    Photographer based in Birmingham, specialising in construction, architectural and commercial work.

  4. Make it relevant to them, not to you

    Reference their project and the photography angle that fits it (progress documentation, completion photos, case-study images). Pitch support for the project — never “I found your government contract.” Don't quote award numbers or contract values in the email.

  5. Include a working portfolio link

    Test the link before sending. Point to the construction / architecture gallery if you have one.

  6. Make contact easy

    Business email and phone in the signature.

  7. Ask one simple question

    “Do you currently have a photographer documenting progress on this project, or is that handled internally?” A yes/no question is easy to answer.

  8. Follow up once or twice, politely — then stop

    Suggested rhythm (a recommendation, adjust as needed): Follow-up 1 about a week later, Follow-up 2 about two weeks after that, then mark No response and move on. Don't chase repeatedly; a polite record keeps the door open for the next project.

  9. Stay professional, and don't take silence personally

    Many primes handle photos internally or already have a vendor. Consistent, respectful outreach across many companies is what produces the few that say yes.

Principles adapted from Philip Mowbray, “How to write prospective emails to photography clients,” Picfair Focus, translated here for federal-construction outreach to prime contractors.

Method

How the construction prospect list was built

    How the photography angle and signal are assigned (ANALYTICAL rules)
    • New construction, design-build, construction management: progress photography, construction documentation, project milestones; add completion and architectural photography when the work is a building rather than equipment or utilities.
    • Repair / renovation: site (before/after) and construction documentation; add facility and completion photography for building renovations.
    • Corporate / case study, marketing content: only for building projects of $20M or more, where builders commonly want portfolio imagery.
    • Public affairs: only for public-facing sites (national cemetery, interpretive center, courthouse, national monument) and not for equipment-replacement jobs.
    • Aerial / drone: only for outdoor, non-restricted sites (cemetery, lock, bridge, park). Never suggested for Redstone Arsenal, NASA Marshall, the FBI campus, Maxwell/Gunter, Fort Novosel, Anniston Army Depot or VA medical centers. Commercial drone work needs FAA Part 107 certification and the site owner's permission.
    • Low signal: dredging, equipment or utility replacement (boilers, chillers, roofs, fire alarms, paving, sewer, windows) and jobs under $1M. Still listed, because the company may do photographable work elsewhere.
    • Verified photo subaward: only where the prime reported a photography subcontract — one project.
    Corrections made in the client data layer
    • New Anniston Federal Courthouse (47PE0318F0004): the workbook's site column reads "Anniston Army Depot / Anniston"; the award description names the courthouse, so the site is shown as the courthouse.
    • EHRM upgrades (36C77623C0164): place of performance is coded Alabaster (the contractor's office); the description names the Birmingham VA Medical Center.
    • Rows whose place-of-performance city equals the contractor's office city keep an "unconfirmed site" note.
    • Two contracts coded to Alabama but performed elsewhere (a U.S. Embassy project and one other) were already excluded in the workbook.

    Limits

    Known limitations

    • Photography need is not documented for any project except the Anniston courthouse subcontract. Every angle on this site is an outreach hypothesis.
    • Primes only report subcontracts of $30,000 or more, so small photo packages don't appear in federal data.
    • Defense Department records reach USAspending about 90 days late; end dates move when contracts are modified.
    • Company contact paths were checked on 2026-09-21. Vendor portals change — confirm on the company's site.
    • Controlled sites (Redstone Arsenal, NASA Marshall, the FBI campus, Maxwell, Fort Novosel, Anniston Army Depot, VA hospitals) require the prime to arrange access and photo authorization.
    • Outreach tracking is stored in one browser only. Export a backup.
